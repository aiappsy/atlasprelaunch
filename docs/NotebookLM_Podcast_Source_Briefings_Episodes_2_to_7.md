### 1. Demystifying Travel Distribution: Why Wholesale Exists
To understand ATLAS Travel Club, one must understand how travel distribution actually works beneath the consumer-facing internet.

Most consumers interact with travel exclusively through public Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) such as Booking.com or Expedia. These platforms spend tens of billions of dollars annually on pay-per-click Google search ads and television branding. To recoup these massive acquisition costs and generate public market margins, public OTAs charge hotels and accommodation providers commissions typically ranging from 15% to 30%.

Simultaneously, hoteliers are legally bound by "Rate Parity" agreements on open public channels—meaning they cannot publicly display lower rates on their own website without risking algorithmic penalization from major booking engines.

However, international trade regulations and competition law provide a clear, recognized exemption: **closed-user group memberships**. When rates are placed behind an authenticated, paid membership paywall (the classic Costco model), hotels, bedbanks, and airlines are permitted to distribute inventory at net wholesale rates that are never exposed to public search crawlers.

### 2. The Flight Infrastructure: Duffel NDC Integration
Airlines have historically distributed inventory through Global Distribution Systems (GDS) like Sabre and Amadeus. These legacy intermediaries impose distribution fees, segment charges, and technology surcharges that OTAs pass on to passengers with additional booking markups.

ATLAS approaches flight booking by partnering with **Duffel**, the next-generation airline technology platform:
* **Direct NDC Connections:** Duffel integrates directly with airlines' New Distribution Capability (NDC) APIs—including British Airways, Lufthansa Group (Swiss, Austrian, Brussels Airlines), SAS, Air France-KLM, Emirates, and United.
* **Pure Net Carrier Fares:** When a member searches for a flight on ATLAS, the platform pulls real-time NDC seat inventory directly through Duffel's API. ATLAS adds **0 kr / €0 in ticket markups**.
* **Transparent Ancillaries:** Baggage allowances, seat selection, and cabin upgrades are displayed at direct airline cost without OTA price inflation.
* **Realistic Savings Context:** It is vital for members to understand the economics of aviation. Airlines operate on tight operational margins (typically 2% to 6%). ATLAS does not claim to make flights 50% cheaper. Instead, Duffel eliminates GDS booking surcharges, payment processing fees, and OTA profit margins, producing realistic, consistent savings of **5% to 14% (typically €15 to €40 per ticket)**.
